The goal is to create a system that can rate managers in such a way to give insights to teams on how managers truly affect their teams and their success. As much attention has been primarily focused on analyzing players, at least publicly, there has been no attempts made to analyze coaches on an objective, comparative basis. The current, initial plan is to somewhat brute force an initial model that makes sense to me, or at least to see if there is any merit to the idea at all. The idea is to scrape online data for estimating team transfer value, accounting for age and position bias, and then create a ranking system for every league. This will say what the expected finishing position for every team in the league will be based on their estimated total transfer value accounting for age, position, and playing time. Essentially, what we hope this adjusted estimated transfer value will tell us, is how good on average a team is. We adjust for age and position because these factors affect transfer value independent of how effective the player truly is. We adjust for playing time because that way we can effectively rate the squad that is on average, on the pitch. We will then compare this ranking to where teams actually finish for these given years, and attribute the difference to the coaching. This, hopefully, will at least give us a rough proof of concept that there is a sort of correlation in this data at all. Then, we will attempt to take this measure of team value, along with many other indicators of team success, such as xG, goals, assists, take ons, and other statistics, and attempt to have a program create a more refined formula for a “coach factor”. This will hopefully be a number we can give every coach to assign a sort of value to how well a coach can improve a team’s likelihood of winning games or placing higher. 
This, however, is not the only value of a coach. Another large value of a coach is player development. Some teams’ profit strategy is not to sell jerseys, win tournaments, and gain international fame, but rather to buy up promising players, develop them further, and then sell them for a premium to large clubs. This means, that rather than a score for how well coaches improve teams’ winning potential, these clubs would want a score for how effectively a coach can increase their players’ transfer value. Unlike the earlier adjustments, we will not adjust based on age or position, because we do not truly care about anything other than pure transfer value of these players. We will, however, discount decreases in player value if the players are over a certain age because these players are likely there not in order to grow in value, but to provide mentorship, leadership, and guidance to the younger players. We will then look at player values after each season and mark percentage deltas, adjusting as mentioned, in transfer value. This, however, will not be perfect, as it is obvious that already extremely valuable players are not likely to have huge percentage increases in value, whereas any team with a random breakout superstar can have a huge increase in value in one year. This means that we will likely have to remove the top increaser from each team to remove one offs, and we will also have to increase the value of transfer value gains for already valued players. This, all put together, should hopefully give a rough estimate of how well coaches increase players’ transfer values.
One more thing that may be useful for teams to know, is coaching style, or rather what type of soccer coaches want their teams to play. This is useful largely because of the transition period between coaches. When hiring a new coach, it is necessary for clubs to acknowledge that there will likely be an acclimating period as the players have to learn to play in the new coaches’ system. This period can be shortened by hiring a coach with a similar style to the existing coach. Or, some teams may want the opposite, a complete turn around in coaching style in order to reinvigorate the team, or to allow certain players to shine, develop, or just change the clubs direction. We hope to estimate playstyles by feeding a program pass charts, heat maps, xG charts, take ons, tackles, and many other statistics to then categorize teams into rough categories. We will then look at these categories and assign them names, either based on currently known styles, or looking at connections previously unseen. This should hopefully allow us to see teams that play similarly and identify what makes each style unique.
Altogether, these should create at least a rough basis for measuring coaches through several different “Advanced Coaching Statistics”. We can combine these for a rough overall score, just to give a big fancy number to draw attention, either on social media, or to large teams who may be interested in this product.
